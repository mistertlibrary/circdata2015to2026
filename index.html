Our Circ Data: Three Visualizations

Books borrowed from the West Essex Regional High School Library, 2015–16 to 2026–27

We shape our data, then our data shape us. Below, the same twelve years of library data appear three ways, each shamelessly stolen from an innovative designer or design team. Toggle between them. Notice what each makes obvious and where each falls short.

The spiral, after W.E.B. Du Bois

Books borrowed from the library of West Essex Regional High School

By school year, 2015–16 to 2026–27

ONE FULL TURN = 2,500 CHECKOUTS · DASHED RING = PARTIAL YEAR

About this form

For the 1900 Paris Exposition, W.E.B. Du Bois and students at Atlanta University drew dozens of charts by hand to document Black life in Georgia and the United States. Some of his most striking charts bend long bars into spirals, so very large and very small numbers can share one page.

Look closer
  • Which ring is shortest? What was happening that school year?
  • Is a curved line easier or harder to compare than a straight one?

The pictograms, after Otto Neurath

What WEHS borrowed, year by year

Each book = 50 checkouts · faded row = partial year

About this form

In 1920s Vienna, Otto Neurath, Marie Reidemeister, and Gerd Arntz built a pictorial language for statistics so ordinary people could "read" key data reflecting their society. Their method, later called Isotype, builds every chart from repeating symbols that always stand for the same quantity.

Look closer
  • Count the symbols in one row. How close does your count come to the real total?
  • Which category grew over twelve years, and which shrank?

The postcards, after Dear Data

one dot = 10 checkouts · postmark = books returned · dashed card = partial year

About this form

For one year, designers Giorgia Lupi and Stefanie Posavec mailed each other a postcard every week, each one a hand-drawn picture of data about their own lives. Their project, Dear Data, treats data as a portrait of people rather than a column of numbers.

Look closer
  • Find a card whose postmark is larger than its count. Why might more books come back than the quantity that went out?
  • If you drew your own card, what would your year of reading look like?
The numbers behind every view
Checkouts by category and check-ins by school year

About the data

These figures come from the library's circulation statistics. They only count books (our library's most popular holdings are, alas, loaner Chromebooks). A school year runs from July 1 to June 30, and 2026–27 is partial, tallied only up to November 18, 2026. “Other” gathers short stories, graphic biographies, vintage biographies, reference, and back-office items.
